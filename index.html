      Arriving at Bandaranaike Airport (CMB): A Complete Guide to Arrivals & Private Transfers

      Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB) at Katunayake is Sri Lanka's main international gateway, about 32 km north of central Colombo. Most long-haul and regional flights land here, many of them late at night or in the early hours. A little preparation makes the first hour on the island calm rather than confusing.

      Before you fly

      • Entry requirements: check the current visa or Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) rules for your nationality and arrange them before departure.
      • Share your flight number with your transfer provider. It lets the dispatch desk follow your actual landing time rather than the timetable.
      • Note your hotel's full address and a contact number — useful on the arrival card and for your chauffeur.

      From the aircraft to the arrivals hall

      After landing you pass through immigration, then collect your bags in the baggage hall. The arrivals area has duty-free shops, bank counters for currency exchange and mobile network counters selling tourist SIM cards — it is worth buying a SIM here so you have data from the moment you leave. Customs comes next, and then the exit into the public arrivals area where drivers and families wait.

      Allow roughly 45–90 minutes from touchdown to the exit, depending on queues at immigration and how quickly bags arrive. Several flights landing together can add time.

      How a meet-and-greet works

      With a pre-booked meet-and-greet, your chauffeur waits in the arrivals area with a name board. They help with your luggage, walk you to the vehicle and confirm the route and any stops before you set off. Because the desk tracks your flight, an early or late landing simply moves the pickup — you are not left waiting, and nobody leaves before you arrive.

      Typical transfer times from CMB

      DestinationDistanceTypical time
      Negombo≈ 10 km15–25 min
      Colombo city≈ 32 km35–60 min via the Katunayake Expressway
      Kandy≈ 100 km2.5–3.5 hours
      Galle / Bentota≈ 150 km2–2.5 hours via the expressways
      Sigiriya / Dambulla≈ 150 km3.5–4.5 hours
      Ella≈ 230 km6–7 hours

      Times are approximate. Colombo's weekday rush hours (roughly 07:30–09:30 and 16:30–19:00) can add significantly to city transfers; overnight arrivals usually move quickly.

      Private transfer, taxi or ride-hailing?

      Ride-hailing apps work well within Colombo, and there are taxi counters at the airport. For a long first journey after an overnight flight — especially to the hills, the Cultural Triangle or the south coast — a pre-booked private transfer means a fixed, all-in price agreed in advance, a vehicle sized for your luggage, and a chauffeur who knows the route and can stop when you need to.

      Practical tips

      • Carry a little Sri Lankan rupee cash for tips and small purchases; cards are widely accepted in hotels and larger shops.
      • Tell your transfer provider about child seats, extra luggage, surfboards or golf bags when you book.
      • If you are heading straight into the hills, a break-the-journey stop (such as Ethagala on the way to Kandy) turns the transfer into part of the trip.

      Colombo to Kandy, Sigiriya & Ella: Private Chauffeur vs Train, Bus & Taxi

      The classic first-time route runs from Colombo or the airport to Kandy, north to Sigiriya and the Cultural Triangle, then up into the tea country to Nuwara Eliya and Ella. Each way of covering it has real strengths — and the best plans often mix them.

      The train: unforgettable scenery, real logistics

      The hill-country line between Kandy and Ella is one of the world's great train rides, winding through tea estates, tunnels and cloud forest. It is slow — the Kandy–Ella stretch takes around 6–7 hours — and reserved seats in the observation and first- or second-class carriages sell out well ahead in peak season. Unreserved carriages can be very crowded. Stations are often some distance from hotels, and managing large suitcases on and off the train is hard work.

      The train does not reach Sigiriya, so the Cultural Triangle needs road transport anyway.

      Buses: cheapest, but not for luggage or schedules

      Public buses connect almost everywhere for very little money, and they are a genuine local experience. They are also crowded, driven briskly, and have little space for luggage. For travellers on a tight schedule or with families, they are rarely the right choice for long legs.

      Taxis and ride-hailing

      Ride-hailing apps and tuk-tuks are excellent for short hops within Colombo and around towns. For long intercity journeys, availability is less predictable, and drivers may not know the sights along the route.

      A private chauffeur

      A private vehicle with a chauffeur gives you door-to-door travel from hotel to hotel, room for luggage, air-conditioning and the freedom to stop at a spice garden, a waterfall or a tea factory on the way. The price is agreed upfront and covers fuel, tolls and parking. On a multi-day trip the same chauffeur stays with you, which means local knowledge, a consistent point of contact and no daily logistics to solve.

      Typical road times

      RouteBy road (approx.)
      Colombo → Kandy3–3.5 hours
      Kandy → Sigiriya2.5–3 hours
      Sigiriya → Nuwara Eliya4.5–5 hours
      Kandy → Nuwara Eliya2.5–3 hours
      Nuwara Eliya → Ella2–2.5 hours
      Ella → Colombo (via expressway)5–6 hours

      The best of both: chauffeur plus one train leg

      Many travellers keep the chauffeur for the whole trip and ride the most scenic section by train — typically Nanu Oya (for Nuwara Eliya) to Ella, about 3–3.5 hours. Your chauffeur drops you at the station, drives your luggage ahead, and meets you on the platform at the other end. You get the views without hauling suitcases, and the car is there for everything the railway does not reach.

      A sample 6-day loop

      1. Airport → Sigiriya area (via Dambulla Cave Temple)
      2. Sigiriya at sunrise, Polonnaruwa in the afternoon
      3. Sigiriya → Kandy (spice garden en route); evening at the Temple of the Tooth
      4. Kandy → Nuwara Eliya via tea estates and Ramboda Falls
      5. Nanu Oya → Ella by train; chauffeur meets you in Ella
      6. Ella → Colombo or the south coast

      Executive & Corporate Chauffeur Standards in Colombo: A Guide for Travel Managers

      For a visiting executive, the car is often the first and last impression of a trip. Whether you are arranging a single airport pickup for a director or moving a delegation between hotels, meetings and an event venue, the same standards separate a reliable provider from a risky one.

      1. Punctuality built into the plan

      Good providers plan around Colombo's traffic rather than react to it. Weekday peaks (roughly 07:30–09:30 and 16:30–19:00) and road closures for events can double journey times across the city. Expect the dispatch desk to time pickups with a margin, track inbound flights, and confirm every movement the evening before.

      2. Vetted, presentable chauffeurs

      Ask how chauffeurs are selected and checked, whether they are experienced on airport and city routes, and how they are briefed. For executive work, chauffeurs should be well presented, courteous, comfortable in English and trained to be discreet: no phone calls during the journey, no discussion of passengers or their business, and confidential documents never left visible in the vehicle.

      3. The right vehicle for the role

      • Executive sedan — one or two passengers, city meetings and airport runs.
      • SUV or luxury vehicle — senior executives, VIP guests and longer outstation travel.
      • Electric or hybrid — quiet, low-emission city travel for companies with sustainability goals.
      • Vans, minibuses and coaches — delegations, site visits, roadshows and events.

      4. Insurance, safety and backup

      Confirm that passengers and vehicles are comprehensively insured, that vehicles are well maintained, and — critically — what happens if something goes wrong. A provider with a 24/7 operations desk can send a replacement vehicle or re-route a chauffeur when a flight changes at midnight.

      5. Delegations and events

      For groups, share a passenger manifest with names, flights and hotels. A single coordinator on the provider's side should own the movement plan, stagger airport pickups by arrival time, and keep a vehicle in reserve on event days.

      6. Clear billing

      Corporate accounts should offer agreed rates, consolidated statements and proper invoices, so that travellers never handle cash and finance teams can allocate costs to the right project or cost centre.

      A checklist for your next booking

      • Passenger name, mobile number and flight number
      • Pickup and drop-off addresses, with meeting times
      • Vehicle class and number of passengers and bags
      • Any security, confidentiality or dress requirements
      • Billing details or corporate account reference
      • An on-the-day contact on your side

      When to Visit Sri Lanka: A Season-by-Season Route Planner

      Sri Lanka's weather is shaped by two monsoons that affect different sides of the island at different times. That is good news: almost every month, one coast is dry and sunny. The trick is choosing the route to match the season.

      December to April: the south and west at their best

      The north-east monsoon brings rain to the east and north, while the west and south coasts enjoy their dry season. This is peak time for Galle, Bentota, Mirissa and the south coast, whale watching off Mirissa (roughly November to April), and clear views in the hill country from January onwards. The Cultural Triangle is pleasant too.

      May to September: the east coast and the north

      The south-west monsoon arrives on the west and south coasts and the western hills, while the east coast — Trincomalee, Pasikudah and Arugam Bay — and the north around Jaffna are dry. Arugam Bay's surf season peaks in these months. The Cultural Triangle stays largely dry, and the elephant gathering at Minneriya is usually at its height around August and September.

      October and November: the inter-monsoon

      Afternoon showers are possible island-wide, but mornings are often clear, prices are lower and sights are quieter. Plan flexible days and early starts.

      Wildlife and festival dates to know

      • Yala National Park: excellent from February to July; the main block usually closes for part of September and October — dates are announced each year.
      • Minneriya and Kaudulla: elephant herds gather on the reservoir shores in the dry months.
      • Kandy Esala Perahera: a spectacular procession held in July or August, with dates set by the lunar calendar.
      • Sinhala and Tamil New Year: mid-April — a festive time, with many locals travelling.

      Planning a route by season

      Travelling inSuggested route
      December – AprilAirport → Cultural Triangle → Kandy → tea country → Ella → Yala → south coast
      May – SeptemberAirport → Cultural Triangle → Kandy → east coast (Trincomalee or Pasikudah), or north to Jaffna
      October – NovemberA shorter loop with flexible days: Kandy, the Cultural Triangle and one coast

      Travelling by road in any season

      Rain rarely stops road travel, but it can slow mountain roads and shift the best time of day for sights. A private chauffeur can rearrange the day around the weather — swapping a morning hike for a tea factory, or leaving early to beat afternoon showers.

      Who we are

      This website is operated by Airport First (Pvt) Ltd, WTC West Tower, 37th Floor, World Trade Center, Colombo 00100, Sri Lanka (“Airport First”, “we”). We are responsible for the personal information you share with us through this site.

      What we collect

      • Inquiries: your name, phone or WhatsApp number, email address, pick-up and drop-off locations, travel date and time, flight number, passenger count, vehicle preference and any notes.
      • Reviews: your name, country, rating, review text and, if you choose, a small photo. Approved reviews are shown publicly on this site.
      • Messages: anything you send us by email, phone or WhatsApp.
      • Place search: as you type in the pick-up or drop-off field, the text is sent to Photon, an OpenStreetMap-based search service run by komoot, to suggest matching places in Sri Lanka. Nothing else from the form is sent.
      • Route distance: once both places are chosen, their map coordinates (not your name or contact details) are sent to the public OSRM routing service to estimate the driving distance and time.
      • Location (optional): only if you tap “use my current location”. Your coordinates are sent to OpenStreetMap’s Nominatim service to find the nearest address, which is placed in the pick-up field for you to check.

      How we use it

      To reply to your inquiry, prepare a quote, arrange and carry out your transport, share the necessary trip details with the chauffeur assigned to you, and meet our legal and accounting obligations. We do not sell your information or use it for third-party advertising.

      Where it is kept

      Inquiries are emailed to our dispatch team through our own mail server and stored in a secure cloud database (Supabase). Access is limited to authorised Airport First staff. If you choose to contact us on WhatsApp, that conversation is also subject to WhatsApp’s own terms.

      Third-party services

      To display this site, your browser loads fonts from Google Fonts, images from Unsplash and scripts from jsDelivr and Tailwind CSS. These providers receive your IP address as part of a normal web request. We do not use advertising or analytics cookies; the site uses your browser’s storage only for essential functions.

      How long we keep it

      For as long as needed to handle your inquiry and any resulting booking, and to meet legal and accounting requirements. After that it is deleted.
